## [Aegis: Runtime Governance for Agentic AI with Fail-Closed Execution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16891) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Aegis is a new runtime governance system that treats model outputs as action proposals and mediates them through a trusted decision layer before tool execution. In a sandbox corpus of 6,300 rows, it reduced risky actions to zero, with all governed rows preserving trusted provenance. This addresses a critical safety gap in agentic AI, where prompt-level governance is insufficient to prevent harmful operational side effects. By enforcing action boundaries at runtime, Aegis could enable safer deployment of autonomous agents in regulated and safety-critical environments. Aegis uses a Senate-style settlement mechanism for non-unilateral authorization, requiring quorum and signed tally evidence. The evaluation showed zero governed risky side-effect completions across 2,100 Aegis-governed rows, but the authors caution that results do not prove general autonomous-agent safety.

rss · arXiv - AI · Aug 19, 04:00

**Background**: Agentic AI systems can take actions like modifying files or sending messages, shifting safety concerns from text generation to operational side effects. Traditional prompt-level governance shapes model behavior but does not create an execution boundary, so runtime governance is needed to enforce policies at execution time. Fail-closed execution ensures that actions are withheld unless explicitly permitted, which is crucial for safety-critical environments.
