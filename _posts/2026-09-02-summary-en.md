## [LLM Long-Horizon State Tracking Tested via Step-by-Step MD5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00012) ⭐️ 8.0/10

This paper introduces a benchmark where LLMs compute MD5 step-by-step through 196 dependent tool calls over 64 rounds, isolating long-horizon state tracking from instruction interpretation. The model gpt-oss-120b successfully carries the full state across all calls and returns the correct digest on a majority of completed runs. This work addresses a critical gap in LLM evaluation by providing a clean, controllable test for state tracking, which is essential for agentic systems that rely on multi-step tool use. The findings could guide improvements in model architecture or prompting strategies to reduce error cascades in long-horizon tasks. The benchmark implements MD5 from scratch (RFC 1321), aligning every call to the ground-truth trace and checking the digest bit-by-bit, so any failure is purely due to bookkeeping. In the strongest setting, every primitive tool is replaced with a second LLM, removing any exact-arithmetic oracle, and success depends on keeping the model's reasoning in context and voting over a thinking-enabled worker.

rss · arXiv - AI · Sep 2, 04:00

**Background**: MD5 is a widely used cryptographic hash function that produces a 128-bit hash value, designed by Ronald Rivest in 1991 and specified in RFC 1321. Long-horizon tasks in LLMs are challenging because errors can cascade over many dependent steps, and existing benchmarks often confound state tracking with instruction interpretation, lacking control groups to isolate the core difficulty.

## [SCAFFOLD: Large-Scale Dataset for CS Diagram QA with CoT Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00018) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers introduced SCAFFOLD, a large-scale structured dataset of computer science research figures, containing (image, caption, context, question-answer, chain-of-thought) tuples. It includes three versions: SCAFFOLD-157K (157,387 pairs from 29,887 figures in 3,058 papers), SCAFFOLD-37K (36,797 pairs), and SCAFFOLD-12K (12,000 pairs), with baseline experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct. This dataset fills a critical gap in vision-language research by providing a dedicated resource for understanding technical diagrams in computer science papers, which often convey more information than text. It enables training and evaluation of vision-language models on diagram QA with chain-of-thought reasoning, potentially improving their ability to interpret complex figures in academic literature. The dataset was constructed using layout detection and PDF parsing from arXiv computer science papers, with an AI-assisted question-generation step. The largest version, SCAFFOLD-157K, spans 3,058 papers with 29,887 figures, while the smallest, SCAFFOLD-12K, was used for baseline experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct.

rss · arXiv - AI · Sep 2, 04:00

**Background**: Computer science papers often use diagrams such as architecture drawings, system flowcharts, and pipeline schematics to convey complex information. Vision-language models (VLMs) need paired data of images with captions, context, questions, answers, and reasoning traces to learn to understand such technical figures. Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning traces provide step-by-step explanations that help models reason about visual content. This dataset addresses the lack of a public resource specifically for CS research figures.
