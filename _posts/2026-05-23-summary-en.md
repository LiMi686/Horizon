## [80386 Microcode Disassembled](https://www.reenigne.org/blog/80386-microcode-disassembled/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A detailed disassembly and analysis of the Intel 80386 processor's microcode has been published, revealing how complex x86 instructions were implemented at the microarchitectural level. This reverse engineering achievement demystifies a previously undocumented black box, providing rare insight into the design of a classic processor and enabling deeper understanding of computer architecture. The microcode was extracted from high-resolution die photographs using AI-assisted techniques, and the binary image was reconstructed by tracing connections on the die.

hackernews · nand2mario · May 23, 12:11 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48247004)

**Background**: Microcode is a low-level layer of control instructions that implement a processor's instruction set architecture (ISA). The 80386, released in 1985, was Intel's first 32-bit x86 processor and used microcode to handle complex instructions. Reverse engineering its microcode requires detailed analysis of the physical die and ROM contents.

## [RuView: WiFi-Based Spatial Intelligence Without Cameras](https://github.com/ruvnet/RuView) ⭐️ 8.0/10

RuView is an open-source platform that uses commodity WiFi signals and ESP32 sensors to detect human presence, vital signs, and activity through walls without any cameras or wearables. This technology enables privacy-preserving sensing for smart homes, healthcare, and security, potentially replacing cameras in sensitive areas while providing comparable spatial intelligence. RuView uses Channel State Information (CSI) from ESP32-S3 nodes, runs on edge hardware with spiking neural networks, and currently achieves limited pose accuracy (PCK@20 ≈ 2.5%) without camera supervision, targeting 35%+ with future ground-truth training.

rss · GitHub Trending - Daily (All) · May 23, 22:45

**Background**: WiFi sensing exploits how radio waves are disturbed by human movement and breathing, captured as Channel State Information (CSI). DensePose is a computer vision technique that maps human pixels to 3D body surfaces; RuView adapts this concept to WiFi signals for camera-free pose estimation.
