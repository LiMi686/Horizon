EXAONE Forecast for Finance (EXAONE Finance) is introduced as a financial time series foundation model that replaces self-attention with linear-time operators: causal 1D convolution for temporal mixing and group-aware pooling MLP for variate mixing. It is pretrained on a large-scale financial corpus and achieves state-of-the-art performance on the FinVerse benchmark across all three evaluation tiers. This model addresses key limitations of existing time series foundation models for finance, such as quadratic computational cost and assumptions of fully observed inputs. Its attention-free architecture and robustness to missing data could enable more efficient and accurate forecasting across diverse financial asset classes, benefiting quantitative finance and AI/ML practitioners. The model uses masked context augmentation during training to handle contiguous missing spans, addressing the pervasive missingness in financial markets. It is pretrained on a corpus covering equities, foreign exchange, commodities, crypto-assets, fixed income, and macroeconomic indicators, and ranks first on FinVerse across point-forecast accuracy, cross-sectional asset ranking, and portfolio profitability.

**Background**: Time series foundation models (TSFMs) are large-scale pretrained models that achieve strong zero-shot performance on forecasting tasks. However, most TSFMs rely on self-attention, which scales quadratically with sequence length and variate count, and they often assume fully observed inputs, which is unrealistic for financial data. Financial time series are typically long, many-channel, and intermittently observed, requiring models that can handle missingness efficiently.

## [Better LLMs Can Increase Systemic Risk in Financial Markets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04373) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new arXiv paper demonstrates that improving LLM capability can increase correlated behavior among agents, leading to higher systemic risk in financial markets. Using agent-based simulations with LLM traders, the authors find that frontier models exhibit more correlated actions as capability rises, creating a non-diversifiable risk floor. This finding challenges the assumption that better individual models always improve system-level outcomes, with significant implications for deploying LLMs in high-stakes domains like finance. It highlights a 'capability paradox' that could affect AI safety and system design, urging careful consideration of shared training and architecture when building multi-agent systems. The study uses agent-based simulations with LLM traders of varying general-purpose capability and finds three main results: (1) frontier LLMs show correlated behavior that increases with capability; (2) when shared reasoning is accurate, more agents reduce market risk; (3) when agents share a common misinformation environment, correlated behavior becomes a liability. The authors propose a general framework showing how correlation creates a non-diversifiable risk floor.

rss · arXiv - AI · Sep 7, 04:00

**Background**: Systemic risk refers to the risk of collapse of an entire financial system or market, as opposed to risk from individual entities. Agent-based simulations are increasingly used to model financial markets with heterogeneous agents, including LLM-powered traders that perceive information, reason, and make trading decisions. This paper builds on prior work exploring LLM agents in financial trading, but focuses on the systemic implications of correlated behavior arising from shared training data and architectures.

## [Corpus Choice Significantly Alters Dependency-Distance Estimates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04223) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new study comparing 38 same-language treebank pairs from Universal Dependencies v2.18 found that dependency-distance estimates vary considerably across corpora, with nearly 40% of pairwise language orderings reversed when substituting one treebank for another. The findings challenge the common practice of treating such estimates as stable language properties. This research has significant implications for quantitative linguistics and cross-linguistic comparisons, as it suggests that dependency-distance metrics are not directly comparable across studies using different corpora. It underscores the need for corpus-aware interpretations and may influence how future typological and NLP research uses such measures. The study employed concordance correlation, Bland-Altman analysis, and a twelve-specification multiverse design, finding that treebank choice accounted for roughly 29% of between-group variance. Despite the variability, all treebanks confirmed dependency-length minimization, with normalized ratios below 1.

rss · arXiv - NLP · Sep 7, 04:00

**Background**: Dependency distance is a measure of the linear distance between syntactically related words in a sentence, often used as an indicator of memory burden and syntactic difficulty. Mean Dependency Distance (MDD) is a key metric in quantitative linguistics, and Universal Dependencies (UD) provides cross-linguistically consistent treebanks for such analyses. This study tests the assumption that MDD estimates from a single corpus reflect a stable property of a language.

## [Distributional Theory Shows Evidence Integration Varies by Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04290) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new arXiv paper (2609.04290) introduces a distributional theory of evidence integration in LLMs, validated over ten million trials across twelve LLMs from four families and eight domains. The theory predicts that evidence persuasiveness depends on receiver priors, and identical evidence can improve weaker models while harming stronger ones. This work addresses a fundamental and timely problem in AI, as LLMs increasingly rely on external evidence from tools and retrieval-augmented generation. The findings could influence future model design and evaluation, particularly in scientific discovery tasks where evidence integration is critical. The theory is driven by a receiver prior weight and a candidate evidence tilt, leading to three predictions. Causal interventions show candidate integration is implemented late in the network, and representations of verification are decodable but have little causal impact on answers.

**Background**: LLMs often reason with external evidence supplied by tools, retrieval-augmented generation, other agents, or users. How they integrate such evidence into decisions they have already begun to form has been unclear. This paper presents a distributional theory to explain this process, validated with large-scale experiments.

## [ASR Hallucinations Traced to Final Encoder Stage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04404) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new arXiv paper (2609.04404) investigates ASR hallucinations as a grounding failure, showing that in two Conformer-Large models (CTC and RNN-T), the final encoder stage is a critical boundary where representations become compact and text-readable, and bypassing it causes garbled outputs rather than fluent fabrications. This work provides a mechanistic understanding of ASR hallucinations, identifying a specific stage in the encoder that is crucial for grounded recognition. The findings could guide improvements in ASR robustness and help mitigate hallucination issues in real-world applications. The study used two independently trained Conformer-Large recognizers—one CTC and one RNN-T—under environmental degradation and speaker-background shift. Bypassing the final encoder block caused divergence on nearly every utterance, while middle blocks had little effect, and the intervention produced garbled or repetitive output, not fluent fabrication.

**Background**: ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition) systems convert speech to text, but sometimes produce fluent text unrelated to the audio, known as hallucinations. This paper views these as a grounding failure where the transcript is not guided by the audio. Conformer-Large is a common architecture combining convolution and self-attention, and CTC and RNN-T are two popular training objectives for end-to-end ASR.

This paper introduces MPS-Bench, a benchmark of 5,181 scenarios across 12 high-risk domains, and reveals that visual information dominates textual risk signals in VLMs, causing them to respond unsafely instead of seeking missing context. The authors propose PRISM, a lightweight input monitor that achieves 0.978 AUC and strictly dominates the safety-utility Pareto frontier. This research addresses a critical gap in personalized safety for vision-language models, which are increasingly deployed in high-stakes settings where context matters. The findings highlight a fundamental failure mechanism and offer a practical solution, potentially influencing future safety evaluations and model designs. The benchmark includes 5,181 scenarios from 584 real-world images across 12 high-risk domains, each paired with a hidden user profile. Evaluations of eight frontier VLMs show they respond directly 86-99% of the time, and none exceeds 2.6/5 on personalized safety. Causal interventions reveal a two-stage mechanism where visual affect transfers into the text stream early, making late-stage remediation unreliable.

Personalized safety aims to address this by having models recognize when they lack necessary context and defer to human input. The concept of visual dominance refers to the phenomenon where visual information overrides textual risk signals during multimodal fusion, leading to unsafe outputs.

This work bridges interpretability methods from deep learning to theoretical computer science, offering a new lens for understanding how algorithms are encoded in computational models. It could enhance AI interpretability and inform research on learning algorithms and mechanistic interpretability. The paper proves that symmetries and path separation in a Turing machine's algorithm induce permutation symmetries and low-rank blocks in its susceptibility matrix. Turing machines are a fundamental model of computation, and deterministic finite automata (DFAs) are a simpler class of state machines. The paper builds on prior work introducing noisy Turing machines, which adds stochastic noise to the computation, allowing for a smooth loss landscape to probe.
