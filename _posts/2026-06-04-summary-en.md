## [New Drug Daraxonrasib Nearly Doubles Pancreatic Cancer Survival](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/06/260604044247.htm) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Scientists have developed daraxonrasib (RMC-6236), an oral RAS inhibitor that targets the KRAS mutation in pancreatic cancer. In a phase 3 clinical trial, the drug nearly doubled median survival to 13.2 months compared to 6.7 months with standard chemotherapy, reducing the risk of death by 60%. This breakthrough transforms a previously 'undruggable' target into a treatable one, offering new hope for pancreatic cancer patients who have had few effective options. It could reshape oncology drug development and improve outcomes for one of the deadliest cancers. Daraxonrasib uses a tri-complex mechanism: it binds to cyclophilin A, which then attaches to active RAS, blocking downstream signaling. The trial was open-label and randomized, and the drug received FDA breakthrough therapy designation in 2025.

rss · ScienceDaily Health · Jun 4, 13:18

**Background**: Pancreatic cancer is highly lethal, with a 5-year survival rate of about 12%. KRAS mutations occur in over 90% of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas (PDAC) and were long considered 'undruggable' due to their smooth surface and lack of deep binding pockets. Recent advances in RAS inhibitors, such as sotorasib for KRAS G12C in lung cancer, have paved the way for targeting other KRAS mutations.

This work addresses the challenging domain of hardware design automation, where strict correctness constraints and long-horizon reasoning are critical, potentially accelerating digital circuit design and reducing human error. The framework uses a Process Reward Model (PRM) to provide dense feedback at each intermediate step, guiding reinforcement-style updates during RAFT fine-tuning, and MCTS to explore alternative reasoning paths for data augmentation.

Generating correct RTL code automatically is difficult because it requires multi-step reasoning and strict adherence to syntax and semantics. Process Reward Models (PRMs) assign rewards to each reasoning step, unlike outcome-based models that only evaluate the final answer. Retrieval-Augmented Fine-Tuning (RAFT) combines retrieval of relevant documents with fine-tuning to improve domain-specific performance.

This research highlights a fundamental flaw in current AI safety mechanisms for autonomous agents, revealing that commonly used intervention triggers are unreliable and that human annotators disagree on when to intervene, challenging the validity of existing evaluation metrics. The study used an 18-dimensional affective-dynamics engine (HEART) to evaluate four trigger families on SWE-bench-Verified debugging traces, finding that threshold-on-state triggers fire on 39-83% of actions due to the saturation trap, and LLM judges achieve at most F1 0.17-0.40 at up to 90x cost.

rss · arXiv - AI · Jun 4, 04:00

**Background**: Autonomous AI agents are increasingly used for long-horizon tasks, requiring runtime safety layers to decide when to interrupt. The HEART engine models affective dynamics to simulate agent frustration. SWE-bench-Verified is a benchmark for software engineering tasks. The paper reveals that intervention timing is a low-reliability construct, with human inter-rater agreement barely above chance.
