## [FDA Approves First Targeted Therapy for Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-class-targeted-therapy-metastatic-pancreatic-cancer) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The FDA has approved the first-in-class targeted therapy for metastatic pancreatic cancer, marking a breakthrough in treating a cancer type that has historically been difficult to manage. The drug targets KRAS, a protein previously considered 'undruggable.' This approval is significant because it provides a new treatment option for patients with metastatic pancreatic cancer, a disease with a very poor prognosis. It also validates the feasibility of targeting KRAS, which is mutated in a substantial fraction of many other cancers, potentially opening doors for broader applications. The approval was notably fast, with the FDA taking just over a month from acceptance of the new drug application (NDA) to approval, compared to the typical 8-12 months. This was enabled by the FDA's CNPV Pilot Program. The drug is a KRAS inhibitor, and resistance mechanisms such as upregulation of receptor tyrosine kinase activity have been observed in some cases.

**Background**: KRAS is a small GTPase that is among the most commonly mutated oncogenes in cancer, driving about 20% of human cancers. Targeted therapy is a type of cancer treatment that specifically attacks cancer cells with certain molecular changes, often leaving normal cells less affected compared to traditional chemotherapy. Historically, KRAS was considered 'undruggable' due to its smooth surface and lack of obvious binding pockets, but recent advances have led to the development of inhibitors.

Amazon announced that Mechanical Turk (MTurk), its pioneering crowdsourcing platform, will shut down on September 30. MTurk's shutdown marks the end of a foundational platform for the gig economy and AI data labeling, reflecting how AI advancements have reduced the need for low-skilled human tasks. Human-in-the-loop (HITL) systems, where humans supervise or correct AI, are now more focused on expert tasks rather than simple microtasks.
