## [Unifying Generative Models via Path Integrals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12438) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This paper formulates generative modeling as a path integral, unifying flow-based, diffusion-based, variational, and adversarial models under a single master action. It introduces diagrammatic perturbation theory with a one-loop correction that reduces error from 53% to 1.6% on solvable and nonlinear drifts. This theoretical unification provides a common framework for major generative model families, potentially leading to new insights and improvements across the field. The dramatic error reduction from the one-loop correction suggests practical benefits for deterministic samplers without extra stochastic sampling cost. The path integral uses the Martin-Siggia-Rose-Janssen-de Dominicis (MSRJD) formalism to separate free and interacting probability flows. Imperfect learned scores are treated as insertions, yielding a response-weighted score-matching objective, and symmetry-equivariant drift design becomes an operator expansion with effective field theory (EFT) power counting.

rss · arXiv - Data Science & Statistics · Aug 14, 04:00

**Background**: Path integrals are a formulation of quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics where the probability amplitude is a sum over all possible trajectories. The Martin-Siggia-Rose (MSR) formalism is a method to write stochastic differential equations as field theories using path integrals. Diagrammatic perturbation theory is a technique to systematically compute corrections to a leading-order approximation using Feynman-like diagrams. This paper applies these physics tools to generative modeling, which aims to learn a probability distribution from data and generate new samples.
