## [ReactionAtlas: ML builds chemical reaction networks from seeds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30778) ⭐️ 9.0/10

ReactionAtlas uses a machine-learned generative model and a DFT-trained machine learned force field to automatically construct chemical reaction networks from a handful of seed molecules, discovering ~47,000 reactions among ~12,000 compounds for prebiotic carbohydrate chemistry. This approach bypasses the traditional slow DFT methods for transition state search, enabling unprecedented scale and accuracy in reaction network mapping, which could transform fields like catalysis, combustion, and origin-of-life research. The MLFF transition states match PBE0 references within 0.5 Å RMSD in 85% of cases, and the framework discovered alternative reaction pathways for the formose cycle, a key prebiotic reaction network.

rss · arXiv - Machine Learning · Jul 1, 04:00

**Background**: Chemical reaction networks map minima and transition states connecting elementary reactions, but constructing them traditionally requires finding tens of thousands of transition states using DFT, which is slow and requires reactant-product pairs as input. ReactionAtlas uses a generative model to propose reactions and a machine learned force field to filter valid transition states, automating the process from seed molecules without hand-crafted rules.

## [Study Reveals Feedback Bottleneck in Multi-Turn Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30774) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new paper introduces a controlled student-teacher protocol to isolate the true impact of natural-language feedback on multi-turn agent improvement, finding that self-generated feedback adds little beyond unguided self-refinement while strong external teachers yield substantial gains. This research challenges the common assumption that self-feedback is effective for iterative improvement, highlighting that the ability to act on feedback—not just feedback availability—is the central bottleneck for interactive agents. The study evaluated thirteen open-weight models across four benchmarks (Omni-MATH, Codeforces, BBEH Linguini, ARC-AGI1) and found that multi-turn improvement often stems from repeated attempts rather than feedback, and that dense interaction matrices show gains are driven more by student ability than teacher identity.

rss · arXiv - AI · Jul 1, 04:00

**Background**: Multi-turn language agents can improve over multiple interactions, but it has been unclear whether gains come from feedback or simply from having more attempts. The paper uses a controlled protocol where student models receive feedback from either themselves (self-feedback) or an external teacher, compared to unguided self-refinement (repeated attempts without feedback). Benchmarks like Omni-MATH test mathematical reasoning, BBEH Linguini tests linguistic analysis, and ARC-AGI1 tests abstract reasoning.

<details><summary>References</summary>

## [SGD Self-Stabilizes at Edge of Stability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30930) ⭐️ 8.0/10

This paper provides sharp convergence guarantees for Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) at the edge of stability, showing that stochasticity causes alternating regimes but self-stabilizes dynamics, enabling convergence with large learning rates. This work bridges a critical gap in optimization theory by analyzing SGD at the edge of stability, a regime widely used in modern deep learning but previously only studied for deterministic gradient descent. It explains why SGD works well with large learning rates, which is essential for training large models efficiently. The analysis focuses on multiclass cross-entropy loss for linear classifiers and two-layer neural networks, proving that SGD returns to stability in a fixed number of iterations. Experiments validate the theoretical findings and highlight benefits of SGD in the large-stepsize regime.

rss · arXiv - Data Science & Statistics · Jul 1, 04:00

**Background**: The edge of stability (EoS) is a phenomenon where gradient descent operates with learning rates so large that the loss oscillates but still decreases overall. Previously, EoS was only understood for deterministic gradient descent, leaving the stochastic case (SGD) unexplored. This paper extends the theory to SGD, showing that stochastic noise can actually help stabilize training.
