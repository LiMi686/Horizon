## [Terry Tao Warns of Severe AI Misalignment in Mathematics](https://mathandai.org/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Terry Tao published an essay titled 'A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics,' arguing that AI's growing role in the field creates a fundamental misalignment, amplified by an Economist article reporting that top mathematicians are outraged by OpenAI's methods. The discussion drew 611 comments on Hacker News, with mathematicians and technologists debating credit, understanding, and the future of mathematical practice. This debate raises fundamental questions about how mathematical credit is assigned, whether AI-generated proofs can be understood by humans, and whether the traditional yardstick of solving open problems still measures contribution. It affects mathematicians, AI labs, and the broader research ecosystem as AI systems increasingly produce research-level mathematics. The controversy centers on AI companies solving famous mathematical problems to benchmark their models, which critics say is detrimental to the science of mathematics; an open letter signed by 25 Fields Medal winners warns of this misalignment. Specific allegations include OpenAI pressuring NYU professor Tristan Buckmaster not to credit a collaborator, and OpenAI's solution to a Millennium Prize problem being overshadowed by claims of scooping and spying.

The Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics, published in June 2026, was an earlier international statement responding to rapid AI progress in producing research-level mathematics. AI systems such as large language models are increasingly capable of assisting with or generating mathematical proofs, prompting debates about ethics, credit, and the nature of mathematical understanding.

## [Human Heart Muscle Cells Found to Regrow After Heart Attack](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260909231754.htm) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have demonstrated for the first time that human heart muscle cells can regrow after a heart attack, overturning the long-held assumption that damage to the heart is irreversible. The pioneering work, led by experts at the University of Sydney, the Baird Institute and Royal Prince Alfred Hospital, opens the possibility of new regenerative treatments for heart failure. This finding challenges a foundational belief in cardiology and could redirect decades of heart failure research toward boosting the heart's own natural repair process. If scientists can amplify this regeneration, it may lead to therapies that restore heart function for millions of patients living with post-infarction heart failure. The study shows that regrowth occurs naturally but at a limited level, so the next step is to identify ways to dramatically boost this repair process. Clinical applications are still years away, and researchers caution that the mechanisms controlling human cardiomyocyte proliferation remain poorly understood.

rss · ScienceDaily Health · Sep 11, 02:56

**Background**: A heart attack, or myocardial infarction, occurs when blood flow to part of the heart is blocked, killing heart muscle cells. Because adult human cardiomyocytes were thought to be permanently unable to divide, the lost muscle was believed to be replaced by scar tissue, progressively weakening the heart and leading to heart failure. Regenerative medicine aims to reverse this by stimulating the heart to rebuild its own muscle, but previous approaches using transplanted stem cells have struggled with poor cell survival and integration.

## [Google DeepMind Releases AlphaGenome API for Genomic Prediction](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphagenome) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google DeepMind has released the AlphaGenome API, a public interface providing programmatic access to its unifying DNA sequence model, along with client-side code, examples, and documentation on GitHub. The API also exposes the AlphaGenome Atlas, a resource of pre-computed variant effect scores and feature importances across the entire human genome, and is offered free of charge for non-commercial use. By opening AlphaGenome to external developers and researchers, DeepMind lowers the barrier to applying state-of-the-art regulatory variant-effect prediction in computational biology, potentially accelerating research into genetic disease mechanisms. The free non-commercial access and pre-computed Atlas could make genome-wide analyses feasible for academic labs that lack large-scale compute resources. AlphaGenome analyzes DNA sequences up to 1 million base pairs long and delivers multimodal predictions at single base-pair resolution for most outputs, including gene expression, splicing, chromatin features, and contact maps. The API is best suited for small-to-medium analyses requiring thousands of predictions, but is likely not suitable for large-scale work exceeding 1 million predictions, and query rates vary with demand.

rss · GitHub Trending - Python · Sep 11, 23:46

**Background**: AlphaGenome is a large-scale deep-learning system developed by Google DeepMind to predict how DNA sequences regulate gene expression and other genomic functions, achieving state-of-the-art results across many variant effect prediction benchmarks as described in Avsec et al. Much of the human genome is non-coding DNA that regulates gene activity, and understanding how variants in these regions affect function is a central challenge in genetics. The API lets researchers query the model without running it locally, while the companion Atlas provides pre-computed predictions for variants across the genome.

## [NCP-ArchPreview scales latent-space language models to 8.9B parameters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10715) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NCP-ArchPreview introduces a latent-space language model that jointly trains next-token prediction (NTP) with Next Concept Prediction (NCP), where the model predicts discrete concepts spanning multiple tokens. It was scaled to 8.9B parameters and trained on 5.73T tokens from the Dolma-3 dataset, marking the largest demonstration of a latent-space language model to date. This work addresses a fundamental limitation of standard autoregressive models by adding an explicit concept-level objective, showing that latent-space modeling can improve efficiency and downstream performance. It could influence how future large language models are pretrained, particularly for reasoning-heavy tasks like math. NCP-ArchPreview reaches OLMo-3-7B's final pretraining loss using only 51.3% of the total training tokens, and after full pretraining it outperforms OLMo-3-7B by 2.45 points on the downstream macro-average, including a 5.99-point gain on GSM8K. The learned latent space also enables lightweight domain adaptation by updating only the 17M-parameter VQ module, and injecting concept representations into a DFlash2 drafter improves mean accepted length by 4.17%.

rss · arXiv - NLP · Sep 11, 04:00

**Background**: Standard large language models are pretrained with next-token prediction, learning to guess the next word in a sequence. Latent-space language models instead learn an internal compressed representation, or latent space, where higher-level units such as concepts can be modeled. Next Concept Prediction extends this idea by having the model predict discrete concepts that span multiple tokens, and product quantization is a vector-quantization technique that builds a large concept vocabulary cheaply by splitting high-dimensional vectors into subspaces.

## [UCLA turns cord blood stem cells into off-the-shelf cancer-fighting T cells](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260909231723.htm) ⭐️ 8.0/10

UCLA researchers engineered cord blood stem cells into ready-made T cells that attack solid tumors using two separate detection systems. In mice, a single dose controlled cancer and extended survival while avoiding a dangerous complication of donor-derived T-cell therapies. If the approach translates to humans, it could enable off-the-shelf T-cell therapies that do not require harvesting and engineering each patient's own cells, potentially making treatment faster, cheaper, and more scalable for solid tumors. It also addresses graft-versus-host disease, a major safety barrier that has limited the use of donor-derived T cells. The T cells are derived from cord blood stem cells and use two separate detection systems to recognize and attack solid tumors, a category that has proven much harder to treat with CAR-T than blood cancers. The results are still preclinical, demonstrated only in mice with a single dose, so safety and efficacy in humans remain unproven.

rss · ScienceDaily Health · Sep 11, 01:08

**Background**: CAR-T cell therapy works by engineering a patient's T cells to recognize and destroy cancer cells, but the standard process is slow, expensive, and personalized. Donor-derived T cells could be made in advance, yet they risk graft-versus-host disease, in which the donor immune cells attack the patient's body. Cord blood is a rich source of young, flexible hematopoietic stem cells that can develop into various blood cell types, making it an attractive starting material for such therapies.
