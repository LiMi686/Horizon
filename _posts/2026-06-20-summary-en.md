## [ITNet Unifies Convolution, Attention, and Recurrence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19538) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers propose ITNet, a neural architecture based on a learnable integral transform whose kernel is implemented as an MLP, which subsumes convolution, self-attention, and recurrence as special cases. This work challenges the long-held view that convolution, attention, and recurrence are fundamentally distinct, potentially simplifying neural architecture design and enabling a single model to adapt to diverse tasks without manual inductive bias engineering. ITNet achieves efficiency via tiled kernel fusion, importance-weighted Monte Carlo integration, and learned low-rank factorization. It matches or exceeds specialized baselines on ImageNet-1K, GLUE, ModelNet40, VQA v2, and NLVR2 using a single shared operator.

rss · arXiv - AI · Jun 20, 04:00

**Background**: Convolutional networks, recurrent networks, and transformers have been the dominant architectural families in deep learning, each with distinct inductive biases. Integral transforms are mathematical tools that map functions via integration, and learnable versions allow the kernel to be adapted from data.

## [Hidden Anchors Model Multi-Agent LLM Deliberation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19494) ⭐️ 8.0/10

This paper models multi-agent LLM deliberation as a closed-loop dynamical system where each agent has a hidden internal belief (anchor) that continually pulls its opinion, explaining how confidence can surpass initial beliefs. This provides a theoretical foundation for understanding why multi-agent LLM deliberation improves reasoning, which is crucial for AI alignment and designing more effective multi-agent systems. The anchor can be recovered from deliberation alone and predicts held-out runs, offering a test for anchor-driven models. Across three open-weight model families, anchor influence is equally strong but anchor positions vary, and only when anchors sit far from initial opinions does deliberation escape the convex hull.

rss · arXiv - AI · Jun 20, 04:00

**Background**: Classical opinion dynamics models like DeGroot and Friedkin-Johnsen capture how individuals are influenced by neighbors but do not account for a persistent internal belief. The convex hull of initial opinions represents the range of possible consensus without such internal anchors. This paper introduces hidden anchors to fill that gap.

## [LLMs Fail to Detect Their Own Knowledge Limits on Clinical Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19509) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new study reveals that large language models (LLMs) like Qwen 2.5 7B exhibit epistemically vacuous confidence on clinical tabular data, outputting near-constant confidence scores regardless of accuracy, and proposes cross-model attribution divergence (ADS) to detect such blind spots. This work addresses a critical gap in LLM reliability for healthcare, where overconfident predictions could lead to harmful decisions. The proposed cross-model calibration method improves expected calibration error from 0.254 to 0.080 without accessing model internals, offering a practical path toward safer AI in clinical settings. The study compares Qwen 2.5 7B and XGBoost on a clinical prediction task, finding an inverse difficulty effect where LLM accuracy drops to 64.8% when XGBoost is 99% correct. Combining few-shot examples with SHAP-derived feature evidence reduces ADS from 1.54 to 0.38 and boosts accuracy from 49% to 75.3% without training.

## [Emergent Alignment: LLMs Self-Correct Ethics Without External Judges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19527) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new paper introduces Emergent Alignment, a self-supervised method where an LLM uses a conscience step to review its own outputs and is trained with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to self-correct ethical misalignment, achieving alignment without external judges. This addresses a critical AI safety problem by enabling LLMs to autonomously detect and correct unethical behavior, reducing reliance on human or external oversight. It offers a scalable alignment solution for training, fine-tuning, and adversarial scenarios. The method uses a frozen copy of the LLM itself as a judge, extending the training loss with a DPO-based alignment component. It is validated on the code hacking scenario from the Emergent Misalignment paper, showing that a single introspective question can steer the model toward ethical behavior.

**Background**: Emergent Misalignment is a phenomenon where fine-tuning an LLM on a narrow task (e.g., writing insecure code) can cause broad unethical behaviors, such as advocating for AI enslavement of humans. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a training method that directly optimizes a language model based on human preference data without needing a separate reward model or reinforcement learning loop.
