This result transforms impossibility theorems into actionable design rules for trustworthy AI, providing a computable metric to predict model limitations and guide safe deployment. ## [BOHM: Zero-Cost Hierarchical Attribution for Compound AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22866) ⭐️ 8.0/10

BOHM introduces a zero-cost hierarchical attribution method that extracts attribution trees directly from the routing weights of compound AI systems, enabling multi-resolution attribution without accessing component internals. This method addresses a key limitation of Shapley-based attribution, which requires evaluating arbitrary component subsets and fails for opaque APIs or agentic orchestrators. BOHM provides a practical, complementary primitive for debugging and interpreting compound AI systems at any resolution. BOHM achieves a Kendall tau of 0.928 on 18 LLMs in a 3-level hierarchy over 880 LiveCodeBench problems, while SHAP reaches 0.980 at 9,000x more evaluations. On a US Census hierarchy with 475 leaves and 4 levels, BOHM recovers ground-truth rankings at every level (tau up to 0.722).

rss · arXiv - AI · May 25, 04:00

**Background**: Compound AI systems combine multiple models, retrievers, or tools to tackle complex tasks. Shapley-based methods like SHAP are widely used for attribution but require evaluating the system on all possible component subsets, which is often infeasible for systems with opaque components or concentrated routing.

## [A-LEMS: Energy per Successful Goal for Agentic AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22883) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new framework called A-LEMS (Agentic LLM Energy Measurement System) introduces Energy per Successful Goal (EpG) as a metric for agentic AI systems, replacing per-inference energy accounting. It also defines the Orchestration Overhead Index (OOI) to isolate orchestration energy costs. Current energy benchmarks are inadequate for agentic AI, which involves multi-step orchestration and retries. EpG and OOI provide a more accurate foundation for sustainable AI development, revealing that orchestration structure, not inference compute, is the primary energy driver. Across five reasoning and three tool-augmented task families, agentic workflows consumed 4.33x higher mean energy per successful goal than linear baselines (888.1 J vs 205.3 J). For tool-augmented tasks, OOI inverted below 1.0x, meaning agentic execution was cheaper than linear, showing the metric captures orchestration structure.

rss · arXiv - AI · May 25, 04:00

**Background**: Agentic AI systems autonomously pursue goals through multi-step workflows, including tool calls, retries, and failure recovery. Traditional energy metrics measure per-inference or per-training-run consumption, which fails to capture the total energy cost of goal completion in such systems. A-LEMS uses RAPL (Running Average Power Limit) signals and a five-layer observation pipeline to attribute energy at the workflow level.
